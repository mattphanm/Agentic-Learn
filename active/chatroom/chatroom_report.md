# Agent Chatroom Report

**Problem**: Make the Court & Stitch website much more customer appealing while staying honest that the business is still in customer discovery and MVP validation.
**Agents**: 3 | **Rounds**: 1
**Date**: 2026-05-17

## Participants
| Agent | Role | Final Confidence |
|-------|------|------------------|
| Agent A | User advocate | 9/10 |
| Agent B | Business strategist | 9/10 |
| Agent C | Frontend / brand designer | 9/10 |

## Consensus
All agents converged on the same direction: the site should feel like a premium editorial drop page first, then reveal MVP validation through early-access and feedback language. The first impression should sell taste, identity, and product desire; the validation mechanics should be framed as helping shape the first release.

## Key Disagreements
There was no meaningful strategic disagreement. The only tension was emphasis: how prominent validation should be. The synthesis is to keep validation explicit, but not visually dominant in the first viewport.

## Recommended Action
Lead with a high-contrast monochrome hero, minimal premium copy, product desire, and a clear early-access CTA. Use the rest of the page to explain the customer gap, show the lookbook direction, and collect MVP signals without claiming proven demand, inventory, or performance.

## Unresolved Risks
The brand still needs stronger source assets and real customer language from interviews. The site should avoid claims like "engineered," "trusted by athletes," "selling fast," or "proven" until customer and product evidence exists.

## Debate Highlights
The sharpest shared line was that customers do not get excited by "we are validating an MVP"; they get excited by a label they want to belong to. The agents recommended language like "Join early access," "Shape the first drop," and "First release in validation" instead of founder-process language.
